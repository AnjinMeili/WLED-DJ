# WLED-DJ: a review, with accuracy measurements and suggestions

For: WLED-DJ's author. From: Anjin, MatrixPrism (a music-reactive LED composer that also streams DDP to
WLED). Reviewed: `haslanli/WLED-DJ` at commit `9e05f5d`. Date: 2026-11-12.

We read the code, ran the test suites, and measured two of the engine's readings (tempo and the harmony
angle) against hand-labelled music sets that we use for our own work. Each figure and code reference was
checked a second time against the run outputs and the code before writing this up. We hope it is useful;
any of it is yours to use.

## What stands out

- **A complete product.** It covers system audio by process tap with no virtual device, analysis every
  10 ms, live stem separation, user looks, a flash limiter, timed DDP output and WLED control, from a
  menu-bar app. Our search found nothing published that combines a process tap, DDP frames and mDNS
  discovery in one macOS app, or that drives lights from live stem separation.
- **Sync by reported latency.** Delaying the lights by the output latency Core Audio reports, rather than a
  manual offset slider, is a thoughtful touch.
- **Security in the studio.** Host, Origin and a per-run token are checked against DNS rebinding and
  cross-site use (`studio.py:36-45, 125-127`).
- **A careful model download.** It is pinned to a commit and checked by SHA-256 and size
  (`stems/models.py:18-23, 54-73`), and the CC BY-NC-SA terms of the weights are stated plainly.
- **Graceful degradation.** Separation falls back to the mix and backs off when the Mac is busy
  (`stems/link.py:29-33, 131-143`).
- **Flash safety.** The flash rules follow W3C's text: the 10% luminance step, the 0.80 ceiling, three a
  second, the 25% area, and WCAG 2.0's working definition of a red flash.
- **Tests.** On our Mac, `uv run pytest` gave 295 passed, 1 skipped (it needs the release helper built), 4
  deselected (hardware, models, corpus), in 143 s; `swift test` 10 of 10 (Swift 6.4).
- **Readable code**, consistent throughout.

## Measurements

Both were run offline through your own `bench/replay.py` `analyse()`, which feeds the real causal
`Analyzer` 512-sample blocks. Predictions were written down before each run, a small pilot came first, and
the runners were reviewed before the full runs. The tally of where the misses land and the share of live
readings were added after the pilot.

### Tempo

The median of `beat.bpm` from 12 s on, counted right within 2% of the label. Sets: 774 previews of the
Beatport EDM Key Dataset that carry a BPM, and 296 loops of the Freesound Loop Dataset (FSLD) tiled to 60 s.
Mix only, since the stem path does not change `beat`.

| | Beatport (774) | FSLD (296) |
|--|--|--|
| WLED-DJ, within 2% | 85.3% | 56.1% |
| WLED-DJ, Acc2 (within 4%, readings at 2, 3, 1/2 or 1/3 of the label also counted) | 88.0% | 68.2% |
| WLED-DJ, share of live readings within 2% | 76.6% | 56.5% |
| QM beat tracker (Davies and Plumbley), within 2% | 95.9% | 63.2% |
| BpmDj, within 2% | 89.8% | 65.5% |
| MatrixPrism's fold (offline, designed on the Beatport set), within 2% | 90.6% | 75.3% |
| Beat This!, within 2% | 87.6% | 58.4% (291 loops) |

The references are offline methods that see the whole preview; WLED-DJ is causal and live, which is the
harder job. Paired on the same tracks (exact McNemar, two-sided, not corrected for the number of
comparisons), QM, BpmDj and the fold are ahead on Beatport (p < 0.001 each). On FSLD the fold (p < 0.001)
and BpmDj (p = 0.002) are ahead, QM by a narrower margin (p = 0.026), and WLED-DJ and Beat This! are level
(p = 0.54). Beat This! was not paired on Beatport.

- **Where it shines:** four-to-the-floor music, 88–98% in the house, techno and trance genres with 40 or
  more tracks (Deep House 97.9%, Minimal 98.1%, Progressive House 98.0%, Techno 88.0%).
- **Where there is most to gain:** Drum & Bass 27.5%, Hip-Hop / R&B 63.3%, Breaks 71.4%, Dubstep 73.2%.
- **Where the misses land:** of Beatport's 114 misses, 34 read at 4:3 of the label, 18 at 2:3, 12 at 4:5 and
  19 at an octave. Since most are not octave errors, a check for 4:3 and 3:2 relations in the candidate
  scoring may pay off more than widening the prior.
- **Live steadiness:** on Beatport about a quarter of the readings from 12 s on are more than 2% off
  (23.4%; 43.5% on FSLD). In the previews we looked at closely, the tracker moves to a 4:3 reading
  mid-track and back. Some hysteresis before switching tempo could help the lights.
- **Speed:** 0.043 s of compute a second of audio, one process. The first estimate arrives at 3.0 s on
  every track of both sets.

### Harmony angle

`tone.angle` (the circular mean from 12 s on) against the hand-labelled key of the Beatport EDM Key
Dataset's 1,100 tracks with one clear key. Since a centroid of a key's notes cannot tell a key from its
relative major or minor, each label is mapped to the angle of its key's scale notes on the same circle of
fifths, and a key and its relative count as the same.

| | Within ±15° | Within ±45° |
|--|--|--|
| WLED-DJ, mix, 1,100 tracks | 22.9% | 53.3% |
| WLED-DJ, stems, a seeded sample of 301 (stratified by genre) | 18.6% | 61.8% |
| An angle at random | 8.3% | 25% |
| MatrixPrism's key detector (102 tracks) | 37.3% | 51.0% |
| Beatport's metadata key (576 tracks) | 33.7% | 47.7% |

Paired within ±15° on the tracks each shares with WLED-DJ's mix run (exact McNemar, two-sided), the key
detector is ahead (p = 0.020) and so is Beatport's metadata key (p = 0.011). Both references find the tonic
on about two-thirds of tracks, which the angle is not designed to do.

- **What the angle follows:** on average it sits close to where the tonic triad projects (about 18° flat
  of the key-signature angle for major keys and 16° sharp for minor ones), with a wide spread from track
  to track (resultant length 0.46). So it follows the tonic chord more than the key signature, which is a
  sound basis for colour; "the song's colour" in the README describes it fairly. If you ever want it to
  read the key itself, a key-profile step (Krumhansl–Kessler or Temperley) on the slow profile would be
  the usual route.
- **With stems** the angle clusters more tightly across tracks (resultant length 0.64 on the 301, against
  0.46 for the mix on all 1,100) and moves toward the tonic (30.3% of tracks within ±15° of the tonic,
  against 16.6% for the mix on all 1,100). On the same 301 tracks the mix scores 21.9% and 52.5%: the two
  modes are level within ±15° (p = 0.31) and stems are ahead within ±45° (p = 0.014). How steady the angle
  is within a track was not measured. Note that the stems are read at 44.1 kHz and the mix at 48 kHz, and
  below about 250 Hz the 8192-point FFT is too coarse to place a note within its semitone, so part of the
  difference between the modes may come from the sample rate, not separation alone.
- **Speed:** mix 0.042 s a second of audio; stems 0.41 s a second of audio a process.

## Suggestions

Each from reading the code, with the line; none was found by running into a fault.

1. **Flash limit area** (`flash.py:19, 89, 118`). The limit acts on frames where at least a quarter of the
   pixels rise (`LARGE = .25`), so a look that strobes 24% of the strip, or moves a 24% block round it, is
   not limited. The module's own reasoning, that a strip lights the room, holds for a fifth of it too. The
   README's rule for look authors, "The flash limit applies to whatever you return", would hold fully with
   a per-region or per-pixel check, or a lower area for strips. This one matters most, for photosensitive
   viewers.
2. **Restoring WLED's settings** (`output/wled.py:85-87, 89-94`). `prepare()` turns realtime receive on in
   the controller's saved configuration (`POST /json/cfg {"if":{"live":{"en":true}}}`) and sets `"lor": 0`.
   Putting both back on quit would match the README's "WLED's own state is restored on quit", which
   currently covers on/off, brightness and live mode.
3. **The bar counter at the wrap** (`analysis/rhythm.py:62-66, 107-111`). `_estimate` wraps `phase` with
   `% 1.0`, but only `step` counts wraps into `beat_index`. When the phase pull carries the phase across
   0/1 (a phase within 0.1 of the wrap at an estimate), `bar_phase` shifts a beat until the kick
   statistics re-learn the downbeat. Counting the pull's wraps too would keep the bar.
4. **Detection delay in the beat phase** (`analysis/rhythm.py:41-46, 81, 114-126`;
   `analysis/analyzer.py:90`). Each hop is stamped with its last sample's time while the 1024-point window
   is centred about 11 ms earlier; the envelope is smoothed in `"same"` mode; and `_interp` returns 0.0 at
   the newest index, so the comb scores "a beat now" without the newest sample. A fixed onset-lag
   allowance, measured once, would line the beat up; the sync's `lead` covers processing time only.
5. **`ddp_fast_refresh`** (`output/wled.py:73`, `service.py:391`). The engine picks 77 fps when WLED's
   info reports this field. We did not find the field in stock WLED (upstream at `fffa05a7`, 2026-11-06)
   or in your WLED PR #5967 as it stood on 2026-11-11, so we take it to come from an earlier build of
   yours (our inference from the PR's text). On stock WLED the engine therefore runs at 60 fps, which
   suits its realtime limit (about 66 fps, by the PR's figure). A README line saying so would help users,
   and `tests/test_output.py:51` could note that the fake reports it.
6. **The capture callback** (`Tap.swift:81, 178`). The comment sets the goal that the callback must not
   allocate; each buffer currently dispatches a closure (`converterQueue.async`) from the I/O thread,
   which allocates. A preallocated ring buffer read by the converter would keep the real-time thread
   allocation-free. Low stakes: a stall loses analysis audio, not what the listener hears.
7. **A Swift 6.4 warning** (`Tap.swift:19-24, 39-41`): "forming 'UnsafeMutableRawPointer' to a variable of
   type 'T'; this is likely incorrect because 'T' may contain an object reference", when reading the device
   name and UID into `CFString`. Reading into an `Unmanaged<CFString>?` and taking its retained value is a
   common form [26].
8. **Latency figures** (README:121-123; `Latency.swift:3-6`). The 20 ms and 170 ms are what macOS reports
   (the test encodes two devices' Core Audio properties); we did not find where the about 2 s for AirPlay
   comes from, so a source for it would help. A measurement at the ear, with a microphone and a photodiode
   on one clock, would confirm the sync per output; ITU-R BT.1359-1's window for television picture
   against sound (detectable from about 45 ms of sound ahead to 125 ms behind) is a published yardstick
   [23].
9. **Labelled data for the bench** (`bench/score_detection.py`). The onset references are flux peaks on the
   isolated stems, a sound basis. Human labels would complement them: MDB-Drums [15] (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0,
   audio included) for kick, snare and hats in mixes; Ballroom or Hainsworth for beats; SALAMI, Harmonix,
   Raveform [19] or EDM-98 for sections; and the tempo and key sets above. Each set's terms should be read at
   its source before use.
10. **Citations.** The analysis builds on published methods, and naming them would help readers:
    FitzGerald for the median-filter HPSS, whose p = 2 mask WLED-DJ uses [30]; Ellis for the
    log-Gaussian tempo prior at 120 BPM (he fits 1.4 octaves; WLED-DJ uses 1) [29]; Harte, Sandler and
    Gasser for the tonal centroid, of which the circle of fifths is one part [31]; and Bello and colleagues'
    tutorial for spectral flux with median thresholds [32]. OKLab's credit to Ottosson is already there.
11. **The name.** An Android app called `WledDj` exists on GitHub (`jwindberg/WledDj`). `wled-dj` and
    `wleddj` were free on PyPI and npm on 2026-11-11, if you want to claim them.

## Improvements to consider, with resources

Ideas for taking the readings further, each with where to start. The numbers in brackets refer to the
references below.

- **Tempo between related metres.** Most misses are 4:3, 2:3 and 4:5 readings, not octaves. Methods that
  score a candidate tempo against pulse trains or resonators at that tempo are a place to start: Percival
  and Tzanetakis's cross-correlation with pulses [1] and Böck, Krebs and Widmer's resonating comb filters
  [2]. Grosche, Müller and Kurth's cyclic tempogram [3] folds tempi that differ by a power of two into one
  class; that removes the octave question, so a 4:3 or 3:2 relation shows on its own, though the paper
  does not treat those ratios. Schreiber and Müller's single-step CNN [4] is a learned alternative.
- **Holding a tempo once found.** Published trackers have several ways to do it: Davies and Plumbley's
  two-state model (one state finds the tempo and follows its changes, the other keeps continuity within one
  tempo hypothesis) [5], Stark, Davies and Plumbley's BTrack, built for real time [6], IBT's competing
  agents [7], and BeatNet's particle filter, which also tracks downbeats and metre online [8]. BeatNet's
  paper reports beat F-measures for causal trackers to compare with (its Table 2).
- **The beat's timing.** Onset detectors have a known delay; a fixed allowance, measured once on labelled
  beats, lines the phase up. SuperFlux [9] is a causal onset function made for online use: in online mode
  its peak picking uses no future frames. The paper does not state a delay figure, so it would still be
  measured.
- **Key, if wanted beside the harmonic centre.** Krumhansl and Kessler's profiles [10], Temperley's revision
  [11], and Faraldo and colleagues' profiles derived from electronic dance music [12]. (Faraldo also
  published the Beatport EDM Key Dataset used above; the profiles came from other collections.)
  Correlating the slow profile with a profile per key gives a key; the angle can stay as the colour.
- **Drum hits.** Wu and colleagues' review of automatic drum transcription [13] covers methods and data;
  Vogl, Widmer and Knees's networks [14] are a strong reference; MDB-Drums [15] has human labels with
  accompaniment.
- **Sections and drops.** Foote's novelty curve [16], McFee and Ellis's Laplacian segmentation [17], and
  MSAF, which packages these with their evaluation [18]. For electronic dance music, Raveform [19] has
  hand-annotated beats, downbeats and functional sections (intro, buildup, drop, breakdown and so on) for
  1,423 tracks; it ships annotations only, with the tracks named by YouTube ID.
- **Flash safety.** WCAG 2.2's success criterion 2.3.1 and its definitions of general and red flash [20].
  Its working definition of a saturated-red transition keeps 2.0's R/(R+G+B) ≥ 0.8 test and replaces the
  (R−G−B)×320 change test with a difference of more than 0.2 in CIE 1976 UCS chromaticity; WCAG 2.2
  introduced this, citing ISO 9241-391, and the current WCAG 2.1 text carries it too. ITU-R BT.1702 [21]
  covers broadcast, and the Trace Center's PEAT [22] can check rendered looks (Windows only).
- **Sync at the ear.** ITU-R BT.1359-1 [23] gives detectability and acceptability windows for picture against
  sound; they were found for television picture and sound, so they are a guide for light against a beat,
  not a tested threshold. Apple's header comments for `kAudioDevicePropertyLatency` and
  `kAudioDevicePropertySafetyOffset` [24] describe what each reported figure covers.
- **The real-time thread.** Ross Bencina's "Real-time audio programming 101" [25] sets out why the audio
  callback should not allocate or lock, and recommends lock-free FIFO queues between the real-time thread
  and the rest; Apple's `Unmanaged` documentation [26] covers taking ownership of Core Foundation objects
  that arrive as unmanaged references.
- **Separation quality.** `museval` [27] computes SDR on MUSDB18 [28], to compare the streamed stems with
  StemgenRT's offline output.

## Related projects working on the same problems

Several open projects build on the same research recipe as WLED-DJ's rhythm analysis: autocorrelation
tempo with a log-Gaussian prior at 120 BPM, a comb for phase, and a phase pull. Each made different
choices, and their code is a useful second opinion on the points above. Licences differ (MIT, Apache-2.0,
GPL-3.0, the Unlicense, and one with none), so read them for ideas and check the licence before reusing
code. Read on 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12 at their default branches.

- **akashic-aurora** (`balanced7/akashic-aurora`, `arsenal/keylab/beat_a.py`, Apache-2.0): stamps each
  frame at its window's centre and keeps an `ONSET_LAG_S` constant for the detector's delay, calibrated on
  its synthetic tests (suggestion 4). Its header names the published sources each part builds on: Bello
  et al. 2005, Scheirer 1998, Klapuri 2006, Ellis 2007, Davies and Plumbley 2007, Stark et al. 2009
  (suggestion 10).
- **cosmic-peanut** (`cbuchert/cosmic-peanut`, MIT): a macOS 14.2+ visualiser in Python with uv, system
  audio with no virtual device, plug-in visuals and an offline render harness. Its tempo module
  (`src/tidalviz/analysis/rhythm.py`) cites Stark, Davies and Plumbley and Ellis, and pulls the phase
  harder (0.5 of the error, once a beat). Its flash limiter (`plugins/builtin/src/lib/flash.js`) caps
  rises at three a second.
- **Nagare** (`bungholesurfers/Nagare`, `native/scene/src/audio.rs`, MIT, in Rust): the same tempo recipe
  with a narrower prior (0.9 octaves) and a 0.3 pull, and it tracks the bass's pitch with YIN.
- **hue-beatsync** (`mathisarends/hue-beatsync`, for Philips Hue): handles output latency the other way
  round, firing the lights a `lead` ahead of the predicted kick, tuned by eye (no licence: read only).
- **bsmono** (`dupontinquiries/bsmono`, `apps/bo-suite/bo-controller/src/stem_split.rs`): median-filter
  HPSS at 17 by 17, FitzGerald's own example sizes, with its choices recorded in
  `apps/bo-suite/DECISIONS.md`.
- **flash-guard** (`zz-plant/flash-guard`, the Unlicense): a live WCAG 2.3.1 governor for visualiser
  canvases, with the red-flash test. It applies the 25% to any 10-degree visual field, not to the whole
  screen: frames are cut into tiles and a field-sized window is slid across them, so a small region
  strobing hard is still caught (`src/thresholds.ts:12-13`, `src/analysis.ts:12-15`). That is one way to
  answer suggestion 1; for a strip, the window would be the length of strip that fills a 10-degree field at the viewing
  distance.
- **Auralis** (`sesyugin/SR-WLED-audio-server-mac`, GPL-3.0): a macOS process tap feeding WLED, but
  sending audio-sync packets so that WLED draws the effects, the other architecture.
- **LedFx** (`LedFx/LedFx`, GPL-3.0): a long-standing whole product of this kind, with DDP and WLED device
  support.

## More we could measure

If useful, we can run any of these the same way and send the figures: beat phase and downbeat against
labelled beats; kick, snare and hat onsets against MDB-Drums; section and drop timing; stem presence and
the separation's SDR against MUSDB18; light against sound at the ear on built-in speakers, wired, AirPods
and AirPlay; and the flash limit on rendered looks with a photosensitivity analyser.
